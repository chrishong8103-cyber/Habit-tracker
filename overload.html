How the weights move

  • Main barbell lifts, beginner: add weight every session you complete (upper body +2.5 kg / 5 lb, squat and deadlift +5 kg / 10 lb). Miss twice in a row and the weight drops 10% so you can build back up.
  • Main barbell lifts, intermediate: 3-week waves off a training max (90% of your estimated 1-rep max). Reps go down and load goes up each week. If you complete every session in a wave, the training max goes up for the next wave.
  • Everything else: double progression. Start at the bottom of the rep range and add a rep each time you complete it. At the top of the range, add the smallest jump and go back to the bottom.
  • Too easy moves you forward twice as fast. Missed keeps the same target for next time.
  • Body part split: one body part a day, with triceps on chest day and biceps on back day. Each muscle has a weekly set target, and those sets are spread across its exercises. In a week with fewer days, parts are combined; if a combined day wouldn't fit your session length, every muscle's sets are trimmed by the same share, and the week shows how many you got against the target.
  • Flexible weeks: pick different days for any week and the sessions rotate in order (A, B, C, A…) onto the days you choose. A past session you didn't log counts as skipped, so the weight doesn't go up for it.
  • Deload weeks cut the sets and the load so you recover, and they don't count toward progression.
